In 2014, the unthinkable happened: Paul Graham named twenty-eight-year-old Sam Altman the new president of Y Combinator. A young founder in jeans and worn sneakers was handed the keys to the most powerful startup engine on Earth.

Instead of merely churning out delivery apps and photo-sharing platforms, Altman steered the juggernaut toward frontier technology. He launched YC Research, backed nuclear fusion pioneer Helion Energy, and funded cellular longevity at Retro Biosciences.

His philosophy rattled traditional venture capitalists. He articulated his defining thesis:

«People catastrophically underestimate the risk of standing still in an exponential world.»
